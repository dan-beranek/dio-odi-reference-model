# DIO/ODI Research Genealogy

The Research Genealogy Registry records the source object, exploratory process, dated concept-development milestones, and intermediate artefacts, alternative publication manifestations, and archive observations associated with the emergence of Digital Identity Optimization (DIO) and the Ontology of Digital Identity (ODI).

It is a research-process layer within the Reference Model's operational metadata. It is maintained separately from the publication registry, distribution registry, canonical Reference Vocabulary, and Semantic Change Feed.

## Research object

The primary object is the living, author-maintained professional ethnography:

- [Od pultu přes ZUR a PSY až k SEO a copy s ostrými hroty! AI a dál…](https://danielberanek.cz/od-pultu-az-k-seo-a-copy-s-ostrymi-hroty-profesni-bio/)

The object is multimodal and layered. It combines a non-linear professional narrative with interpretive notes, a visual semantic map, a semantic inventory, linked source material, machine-readable instructions and metadata, and an author-maintained changelog. These components together formed the material examined during the exploratory process.

## Exploratory process

The registry describes the process as **AI-assisted secondary exploration of a multimodal professional-ethnography object**.

AI systems produced successive readings and interpretations of the source material. The author examined these readings against the source text and its context. Cross-domain associations, implicit readings, interpretive discrepancies, and algorithmic noise became material for further inquiry and conceptual synthesis.

This was an iterative exploratory process rather than a pre-specified linear sequence. The professional ethnography's changelog records the key transition on 21 May 2026: the broad interdisciplinary text had organically developed into a new disciplinary concept; DIO was given its own place in the structure, work on its Manifest began, and the ethnography was explicitly described as the empirical and asynchronous foundation from which DIO was derived. On 22 June 2026, ODI was articulated as the broader theoretical layer behind DIO.

## Concept-development chronology

The current registry preserves seven milestones sourced from the author-maintained changelog:

1. **25 March 2026** — work on the professional-ethnography text begins.
2. **3–4 May 2026** — interpretive methodology and the visual semantic map are added.
3. **18 May 2026** — the Executive Summary and semantic inventory are added.
4. **20 May 2026** — the AI-use notice and machine-readable agent instructions are refined.
5. **21 May 2026** — DIO is articulated and the Manifest work begins.
6. **22 May 2026** — the methodological reflection is extended with language differences, interdisciplinarity, and implicitness.
7. **22 June 2026** — ODI is articulated as the broader theoretical layer.

The date basis for these milestones is the professional ethnography's own changelog. Individual SlideShare artefacts are dated only where a direct publication date is available; otherwise their chronology remains unassigned.

## Earlier professional and AI context

The article [Marketing pro AI startupy & SaaS: copywriting a SEO pro vývojáře LLM a aplikací umělé inteligence](https://danielberanek.cz/online-marketing-pro-ai-pr-copywriter-seo/) is preserved in an [Internet Archive snapshot captured on 12 March 2025 at 18:37:17 UTC](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312183717/https://danielberanek.cz/online-marketing-pro-ai-pr-copywriter-seo/). A second archived copy at [Archive.today](https://archive.is/Bq8tr) corroborates the archived article. The exact original publication date is not established; the article was online **no later than 12 March 2025**. The current article page displays **21 October 2026**, which the author confirms is the date of the latest update, not the original publication date.

The article is relevant to the genealogy because it already formulates a reciprocal relationship between professional expertise and AI:

- **What professional expertise can contribute to AI products:** SEO and discoverability, brand positioning, copywriting and product explanation, journalism and PR, psychology, customer perspective, and the ability to bridge technical domains with audience understanding.
- **What AI systems can contribute to professional work:** assistance with research, writing, synthesis, and information handling, alongside model-specific limitations in Czech-language output, context retention, factual sourcing, consistency, and instruction following.

This is a pre-DIO professional and conceptual precursor. It documents an existing line of inquiry into the relationship between human professional capabilities and AI systems before the DIO articulation on 21 May 2026. It is linked as preceding context for the later exploratory environment, not substituted for the multimodal professional ethnography that the changelog explicitly identifies as the empirical and asynchronous foundation from which DIO was derived.

The article's temporal fields are kept separate: its exact original publication date remains unknown; the earliest archived capture is 12 March 2025 at 18:37:17 UTC; the second archive is retained as corroborating evidence; and the later last-update date is 21 October 2026. An archive capture establishes that the page existed by that point, not the exact day on which it was first published.

## Registry structure and maintenance

- `metadata/research-genealogy.json` is the canonical structured source.
- `metadata/research-genealogy.schema.json` is its JSON Schema 2020-12 contract.
- This document explains the current record model and curation decisions.
- [Content and provenance reconciliation protocol](research-genealogy-content-reconciliation.md) defines the evidence hierarchy, output/manifestation decision rules, and staged work packages for continued curation.

The registry separates:

- research objects;
- research processes and their methods;
- dated milestones and their source basis;
- platform collections;
- individual exploratory and presentation artefacts;
- inventory leads awaiting a direct item record;
- typed relationships connecting objects, processes, concepts, and artefacts.

Where a source page and uploader information have been reviewed, the registry records the directly observable title, attribution, and content characteristics. Author-supplied URLs may also be registered provisionally before their content is accessible or inspected; those records are explicitly marked as unreviewed. Model attribution is preserved when the source states it and is not inferred where it does not. The source object's changelog remains the primary date basis for the concept-development chronology.

The registry now covers a wider, author-supplied set of multi-platform outputs and three alternative indexes of AI-related professional writing. Records whose contents have not yet been inspected are explicitly labelled as such. The registry does not infer artifact creation dates from archive captures, nor does it assign per-text AI-assistance levels where those were not recorded.


## Outputs, manifestations, and archive observations

The registry distinguishes three layers that may otherwise be counted as separate research outputs:

- **Underlying research output:** an analysis, synthesis, interpretation, or other substantive work.
- **Publication manifestation:** a particular web page, shared AI conversation, Canvas artifact, deployed application, PDF, or SlideShare presentation through which that work is presented. A manifestation may be linked to an existing output when the relationship is supported; a candidate relationship remains labelled as such until the content is compared.
- **Archive observation:** a time-stamped capture of a manifestation in Wayback Machine, Megalodon, or Archive.today / Archive.is / Archive.ph. Multiple captures of one manifestation do not create additional research outputs.

Archive timestamps are preserved in UTC. For Megalodon URLs, the source timestamp and Asia/Tokyo (JST) basis are also recorded before normalization. A timestamp identifies an archive observation, not necessarily the creation, completion, or first-publication date of the underlying work. Capture content remains marked unreviewed until inspected.

Where the author supplied an original platform URL but the artifact's content or model version has not been inspected, the record keeps a provisional title and a content-review status. Similar titles or common topics do not by themselves establish identity between two outputs.

## Thematic clusters and output identity

The E-E-A-T analysis cluster groups related items by the author's contextual identification and their shared research subject. Cluster membership is thematic and does not assert that every item is the same report. The Gemini Share identified as probably corresponding to the existing Gemini 3 Deep Research SlideShare item is registered as a possible alternative manifestation pending content comparison.
