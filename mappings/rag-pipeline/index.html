Purpose

This mapping projects the current DIO Reconstruction Failure Mode taxonomy onto a generic retrieval-augmented generation pipeline. Its purpose is diagnostic: to show where RAG processing conditions can contribute to, expose, or amplify failures of digital-identity reconstruction.

A DIO failure mode is defined by the resulting reconstruction, not by the technical stage at which an upstream defect occurs. The relation between RAG stages and DIO failure modes is therefore many-to-many.

Generic RAG stages

  1. source/corpus formation;
  2. segmentation and indexing;
  3. query interpretation and entity linking;
  4. retrieval;
  5. reranking and context assembly;
  6. generation and synthesis;
  7. output validation and citation.

Related concepts outside the taxonomy

Semantic Vulnerability can be used as a robustness-oriented lens for asking how susceptible reconstruction is to perturbations in retrieval conditions, source availability, ranking, or context composition. Reconstruction Capture is relevant where a small subset of retrieved representations disproportionately dominates reconstruction. Identity Collapse remains a separate phenomenon-level concept.

Iterative and Hypothesis-Conditioned Evidence Acquisition

The existing RAG mapping locates possible reconstruction pressures across pipeline stages. It does not explicitly model dependencies that span successive retrieval and reasoning steps. In agentic retrieval, a working hypothesis at step t may influence the next query, tool call, source choice, or selection of candidate evidence. The returned and selected material then informs a subsequent hypothesis, which in turn shapes later search:

Working hypothesis → next retrieval action → exposed/selected evidence → updated hypothesis → subsequent retrieval action

This is a process-level extension across existing RAG loci, not a new canonical DIO/ODI concept or an additional stage that every RAG architecture must implement.

Hypothesis-conditioned query selectionThe current working hypothesis influences which follow-up questions, tools, or source paths are explored.Changes in evidence exposure may interact with existing failure modes such as Entity Substitution, Merge Failure, or Contextual Displacement, depending on the resulting reconstruction.Hypothesis-conditioned search is normal in agentic systems; it is not itself proof of bias or failure.
Path-dependent evidence acquisitionLater evidence and explored alternatives depend partly on earlier retrieval results and intermediate interpretations.An early referential or contextual error may remain unchallenged or be amplified if later steps inherit its assumptions; other steps may also correct it.Dependence across steps does not establish that the evidence is false or that the final reconstruction is defective.
Evidence-lineage dependenceSeveral retrieved documents, summaries, or agent outputs may inherit a shared source or earlier claim, so their number does not establish independent evidentiary support.This matters when judging whether apparently convergent representations provide distinct support for a reconstruction or claim. It may be relevant to Merge Failure or Entity Substitution only when a defective reconstruction is actually produced.Shared provenance is not inherently defective. Independence must not be inferred from document counts, citations, or agent counts alone.
Cross-agent hypothesis propagationIn a multi-agent configuration, one agent's hypothesis, partial match, or summary may condition another agent's retrieval and interpretation.Apparent agreement may reflect a shared premise rather than independent examination; possible reconstruction consequences remain outcome-dependent.This is a multi-agent special case, not a definition of agentic retrieval. Multiple agents can also provide genuinely complementary evidence.

These are local analytical labels for this extension, not a new taxonomy of DIO/ODI failure modes. Their relations to existing DIO/ODI concepts are conditional and non-deterministic. The relevant DIO failure mode remains defined by the resulting reconstruction, not by the mere existence of an iterative search process.

Relation to the commit boundary

The distinction from Constraining the Commit Boundary is complementary:

  • This extension examines how a hypothesis may shape the evidentiary path before a final identity-changing decision.
  • The commit-boundary paper specifies conditions under which the proposed persistent mutation may be authorized.
  • Neither artifact claims that the other problem is solved by its own contribution. A commit guardrail can constrain a write under its declared evidence contract; this extension does not claim to correct the upstream acquisition process, and it does not propose a complete control or recovery mechanism.

A high confidence score or agreement among several agents is therefore not, by itself, evidence that the supporting retrieval paths were independent. Conversely, evidence dependence alone does not prove that a hypothesis is wrong. The mapping identifies a question to inspect, not a universal verdict on agentic retrieval.

Interpretation boundary

The mapping does not redefine DIO as a RAG framework, treat every RAG error as a DIO failure, assert one-to-one relations between pipeline stages and failure modes, or treat retrieval failure, hallucination, factual error, and source-quality problems as automatic synonyms for DIO concepts.
