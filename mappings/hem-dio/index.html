Application Mapping: HEM Heterogeneity Taxonomy × DIO Reconstruction Consequences

Status: derived application layer introduced in Reference Model v0.3.0 and first included in v0.3.2; carried forward in archived v0.4.0 and current v0.5.0 — archive DOI.
Previous archived release: v0.3.1 — 10.5281/zenodo.23290683.
Boundary: not part of the canonical DIO/ODI Reference Vocabulary; not a claim of taxonomic equivalence or deterministic causality.

Context and rationale

Heterogeneous Entity Matching (HEM) provides a systematic taxonomy of heterogeneity affecting entity matching across diverse data environments. Moslemi et al. distinguish between representation heterogeneity—Multimodality, Format Heterogeneity, and Structural (Schema) Heterogeneity—and semantic heterogeneity—Terminology & Language, Contextual Variability, Granularity & Resolution, Temporal Variability, and Data Quality.

The taxonomy is useful here because it treats heterogeneity as a first-class condition of entity matching. It supplies an external analytical axis for asking how variations in the form, organization, meaning, context, temporal state, resolution, or quality of representations may affect subsequent identity reconstruction.

HEM heterogeneity types describe properties or mismatches in input representations and datasets; DIO Reconstruction Failure Modes describe failures or inadequacies of the resulting identity reconstruction. The relation is therefore many-to-many and diagnostic, not a translation between equivalent taxonomies.

Granularity & ResolutionSemantic Fragmentation; Contextual Displacement; Semantic Vulnerabilitymay contribute to / may increaseDifferent levels of detail can remain referentially compatible while resisting coherent integration or displacing the resolution relevant to the observation context.
Temporal VariabilityTemporal Misalignment; Temporal Discontinuitymay contribute toTime-sensitive differences can yield the right referent in the wrong temporal state or break perceived identity continuity across legitimate transformation.
Data QualitySemantic Vulnerability; potentially multiple failure modesmay increase / may contribute toMissing, noisy, inconsistent, or outdated values can make reconstruction disproportionately sensitive to remaining evidence; no single failure follows automatically.

Analytical bridge

The mapping uses three analytical levels: HEM heterogeneity condition → reconstruction pressure → DIO reconstruction consequence. This prevents an upstream data condition from being mislabeled as a downstream reconstruction failure.

Semantic Vulnerability is especially useful as a bridge concept because heterogeneity need not yield an observable failure. It may instead increase susceptibility to disproportionate change when representation availability, interpretation, ranking, or context changes.

Interpretation boundary

This mapping does not claim that HEM and DIO use equivalent taxonomies, that heterogeneity necessarily produces a reconstruction failure, that an entity-matching model error is automatically a DIO failure, or that any relation in the table is deterministic.
