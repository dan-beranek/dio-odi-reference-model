Purpose

The paper and this mapping form one coordinated research artefact: the paper specifies the proposed architecture, while the mapping formalizes its internal conceptual and functional relations. The Markdown source and JSON-LD and Turtle/RDF serializations expose the same relational model for human inspection and machine processing. This mapping makes explicit the relations among diagnostic inputs, domain-governed constraint binding, mutation-specific propositions, evidence states, two-phase Supervisor, commit invariant, assumptions, and prospective evaluation protocol described in production manuscript v55. Its section anchors follow the 4 November 2026 production source; the corresponding paper is publicly available as arXiv v2.

Diagnostic distinctions → domain-configured constraints → mutation-specific claims → claim-specific evidence eligibility → two-phase authorization → protected commit.

How to read the mapping

1. Diagnostic foundation and constraint binding

The architecture draws on the canonical DIO/ODI vocabulary. Selected referential, semantic, temporal, and contextual reconstruction-failure distinctions inform a partial, domain-governed constraint-binding function ϼΩ. Its output is a domain-configured constraint set V_E, not an automatic inference from SKOS hierarchy.

The paper also gives concrete but explicitly illustrative mappings from selected canonical distinctions to domain-level constraint patterns.

2. Mutation and proposition binding

The Supervisor derives the required proposition set qm from the proposed mutation, its target/effect scope, and the applicable versioned domain policy. The Planner cannot substitute or weaken this set. The authorization record αm binds the derived claims, operation scope, and relevant versions.

3. Evidence admission and claim-specific eligibility

Observations enter quarantine before possible admission to Pactive as UNVERIFIED. That status permits Planner reasoning but is not positive authorization support. A separate claim-specific promotion process establishes eligibility for a particular proposition, role, observation domain, and temporal applicability. The eligible set Pauth(qm, ω) is a subset of Pactive, not a separate physical repository. Promotion excludes recorded shared-origin observations from independent corroboration and excludes shadow-derived claim bases. Authorization-eligible evidence can carry support, contradiction, or unresolved-indication roles; eligibility is not a verdict that a source is universally true.

A separate mutation-specific CheckClaimConflict(q_m, P_auth, P_active, P_quar) returns CLEAR, CONFLICT, or INDETERMINATE. CONFLICT requires a relevant contradiction or prohibitive condition established by authorization-eligible evidence; INDETERMINATE may arise from unresolved indications in UNVERIFIED or quarantined material, or from eligible items that cannot be reconciled under policy. Either prevents PASS. An admission-level conflict is not itself a mutation-level BREACH.

A quarantined item is released only after a recorded policy-designated adjudication or versioned-rule decision, evidence-state version advancement, and a fresh admission check. Release confers only UNVERIFIED status; it does not confer P_auth eligibility. The Planner and raw tool outputs cannot directly alter eligibility metadata.

4. Two-phase Supervisor and commit boundary

Phase 1 evaluates the proposed mutation and, on PASS, issues αm. Phase 2 validates the isolated realized candidate, re-derives qm, checks proposition coverage and conflicts, and verifies scope and versions against a consistent commit snapshot σc.

A protected commit requires EvaluateGraphConstraints = PASS, ScopeValid, and VersionsValid. Status precedence is BREACH > UNKNOWN_EVIDENCE > PASS. UNKNOWN_EVIDENCE leads to HALT_HITL only when policy requires review and a reviewer is available; otherwise it leads to DEFER_UNRESOLVED. Human adjudication requires a fresh authorization decision against current versions. One common retry budget applies across both lifecycle phases.

5. Action-specific authorization and staging isolation

The interface distinguishes READ, STAGE_LOCAL, REVERSIBLE_WRITE, and IRREVERSIBLE_WRITE. A READ result enters quarantine and is admitted against current evidence state without replaying retrieval because unrelated pre-retrieval graph/evidence versions changed. STAGE_LOCAL must complete without shared effects and binds local dependency versions only where explicitly required.

REVERSIBLE_WRITE is authorized only for isolated provisional staging in G_shadow, separated from production identity state, Planner-visible shared identity state, downstream consumers, and all P_auth inputs. Its authorization binds v_S, v_pol, and any separately versioned staging-isolation state. The snapshot includes that state when relevant; version comparison and shadow write are atomic at the staging linearization point. Successful staging advances v_S but does not authorize publication to G_prod. If isolation cannot be verified, the candidate is discarded and any shared-identity effect must use the protected IRREVERSIBLE_WRITE path.

6. Conditional guarantee

The invariant is conditional on seven assumptions: domain-policy adequacy, correct claim-specific promotion, fail-closed authorization, enforced protected-write classification, Supervisor-derived claims, scope/version integrity, and integrity of the recorded promotion basis. The mapping does not claim semantic correctness or detection of unrecorded provenance dependencies.

7. Prospective evaluation

The protocol compares Baselines A, B, and C with a DIO/ODI-informed Challenger. Baseline C versus Challenger isolates the incremental mapping question. The comparison controls the case inventory, domain facts, evidence-promotion process, validator class and configuration, action resources, and decision budget; policy-derived qm differences are reported. Case construction is independent of frozen policy authorship and includes a taxonomy-neutral stratum. Baseline C authors are blinded to the DIO/ODI diagnostic mapping and Challenger policy. Prior DIO/ODI exposure is recorded; stronger inference requires independently constituted author teams allocated randomly or by expertise blocks, with uncertainty reflecting between-team variation.

Task- and policy-family-specific margins for acceptable coverage loss (δ_SRC) and false-commit increase (δ_RFCR) must be justified and fixed before outcomes, together with the uncertainty procedure. The binding success routes are (1) LS > −δSRC and UR < 0, or (2) LS > 0 and UR ≤ δRFCR. The uncertainty procedure must account for the two-route OR structure (e.g. simultaneous bounds or another justified multiplicity-control approach). If RFCR is undefined in either condition, RFCR-based routes are non-evaluable. Correctness, safety, admissibility, resolvability, and benignness use a common independent evaluation reference; PEVR separately assesses each condition’s operational contract.

No experimental results are reported. A generic baseline that matches or Pareto-dominates the Challenger counts against incremental mapping value for the task and policy family tested, not against DIO/ODI as a broader framework.

8. Future-research boundary

Dependency-aware evidence lineage and control of the full hypothesis-conditioned feedback loop are explicitly outside the current architecture and remain future work.
