Application Mapping: DIO/ODI Commit-Boundary Contract × Policy-as-Code Authorization

Status: first included in archived Reference Model v0.4.0 — v0.4.0; carried forward in current archived Reference Model v0.5.0 — v0.5.0.
Live Reference Vocabulary: v0.2.0; the mapping remains outside the canonical vocabulary.
Boundary: this mapping does not claim that OPA, Cedar, or any other policy engine natively implements the commit-boundary contract.

Purpose

This mapping examines how the authorization obligations described in Constraining the Commit Boundary: A DIO/ODI-Informed Authorization Guardrail for Agentic Entity Resolution can be represented, evaluated, enforced, and audited in a Policy-as-Code architecture. Open Policy Agent (OPA/Rego) and Cedar are documented reference points, not preselected or validated implementations.

Evidence usable for reasoning ≠ evidence eligible to authorize a persistent identity mutation. A policy engine can evaluate the request and conditions supplied to it. That does not, by itself, establish the truth of the identity hypothesis, authenticate evidence, discover all material conflicts, or make the later graph write atomic.

Mapping

The mapping proceeds from a DIO/ODI-informed contract obligation to a policy representation/evaluation mechanism and the residual conditions required for enforcement. Relations are analytical, not equivalence claims.

Illustrative request shape

A stable integration contract could carry the proposed mutation, the required propositions and their evidence references, the expected candidate-state version, and the applicable context/policy revision. The following is a conceptual shape, not an OPA or Cedar native schema and not executable policy code:

{
  "operation": {
    "kind": "identity_merge",
    "target": "candidate-graph-mutation",
    "scope": "declared-operational-scope"
  },
  "required_claims": [
    {
      "proposition": "same_legal_entity",
      "evidence_refs": ["evidence-record-1", "evidence-record-2"],
      "evidence_state": "application-defined-state"
    }
  ],
  "candidate_state": {
    "expected_graph_version": "version-id"
  },
  "authorization_context": {
    "observed_at": "timestamp",
    "policy_revision": "revision-id"
  }
}

The field semantics must be defined by the application. A string such as PASS or an evidence reference is not proof by itself: the authorization path needs trusted validation outputs, controlled provenance, and binding to the candidate operation and state.

Worked interpretation: a high-confidence merge

Suppose an agent proposes merging two supplier records and assigns its identity hypothesis a confidence of 0.97. The score can guide exploratory reasoning or prioritization, but it is not, by itself, authorization to change the persistent graph.

A DIO/ODI-informed integration asks whether the operation is in scope; every required proposition has eligible claim-specific support; material conflicts are resolved or explicitly block authorization; configured identity-preserving constraints pass; and the policy and graph versions remain valid at the protected commit boundary.

If a required claim lacks eligible support, the outcome may be deferral or UNKNOWN rather than a low-confidence estimate converted into a permit. If the graph version changes after authorization, the earlier decision must not automatically authorize the stale mutation. Even when all encoded conditions pass, Allow means that the configured contract passed against the supplied input; it does not independently prove real-world identity.

What the mapping does and does not establish

It establishes

  • An analytical bridge from published DIO/ODI commit-boundary obligations to Policy-as-Code mechanisms.
  • A separation between policy decision, enforcement, evidence validation, and atomic graph commitment.
  • Explicit integration gaps that remain even when a policy engine is available.
  • A comparison basis that does not conflate semantic authorization with ordinary access-control decisions.

It does not establish

  • That OPA, Cedar, or any other engine natively implements the MVDIO Supervisor Layer.
  • That an engine validates the truth or provenance of evidence merely because it receives structured input.
  • That a decision prevents stale-state commits or bypass unless the integration enforces those conditions.
  • That DIO/ODI-informed policies outperform an equally informed generic policy design. This remains an empirical question.
  • That the proposed integration has been implemented, benchmarked, or independently evaluated.
  • That this mapping adds a canonical DIO/ODI concept or modifies the Reference Vocabulary.

Interpretation boundary

This is a derived, non-normative application mapping. OPA and Cedar are distinct systems with different request and decision models. The mapping identifies capabilities and gaps; it does not treat their constructs as interchangeable.

The scope is authorization of a proposed persistent identity mutation. The mapping does not model the full hypothesis-conditioned evidence-acquisition loop, downstream mutation propagation, dependency-aware cascade mitigation, or post-commit identity recovery. It does not claim these problems are solved by adding a policy engine.
