Genealogical Foundations of DIO/ODI

From situated practice to digital identity: the professional, intellectual, methodological, and practical experiences through which a distinct way of thinking gradually emerged and was later articulated as Digital Identity Optimization (DIO) and the Ontology of Digital Identity (ODI).

This page provides the interpretive account. The Research Genealogy Registry preserves the corresponding research objects, source records, processes, artefacts, archive observations, and provenance relationships.

A non-linear formation

The intellectual and practical foundations of DIO/ODI did not emerge from a single discipline, professional role, or research project. They developed through the interaction of several partially overlapping fields: retail and direct sales, journalism, psychology, communication studies, copywriting, SEO, web technologies, and the operation of self-owned projects. Evidence: the professional ethnography's account of the non-linear trajectory · its research-object record

This trajectory is better understood as cumulative and iterative than as a linear career progression. Practical experience informed academic inquiry; academic methods and communication theories influenced professional work; and insights gained in practice were carried into subsequent projects and conceptual development. The relevant activities often overlapped in time, and their boundaries do not always correspond to formal job titles or institutional categories. Evidence: professional ethnography — methodological note on the non-linear trajectory

The convergence was not planned in advance as the creation of a new framework. DIO/ODI emerged through the later articulation of recurring patterns that had appeared across different environments and kinds of work.

Temporal discipline. Historical periods are used where exact dates are uncertain or where several activities overlapped. An archival capture establishes that a representation existed in the captured form by that date; it does not, by itself, establish when the underlying text or project was created.

Commercial and customer experience

The earliest substantial practical foundation came from involvement in family retail from the early 1990s. The experience extended from the physical preparation and equipment of shops to day-to-day commercial operations, customer interaction, assortment and stock, suppliers, institutional customers, information systems, and the practical conditions under which a business functions. Evidence: professional ethnography — retail history and early shop operations

This environment required attention to the difference between an explicit request and the need or intention behind it. A customer's words, behaviour, circumstances, familiarity with the product, and readiness to make a decision did not always point to the same thing. Effective interaction therefore depended on reading signals in context rather than treating the first stated request as a complete account of the customer's purpose. Evidence: professional ethnography — retail and customer-interaction account

Later work in direct sales and the operation of Christmas-market trading extended this understanding. Customer communication had to be adapted to the situation, the available time, the product, and the customer's degree of interest. Transaction processing, targeted selling, and more intensive product presentation were distinct modes of work rather than interchangeable scripts. Evidence: professional ethnography — Christmas-market venture, 2012–2016

Repeated interactions also demonstrated that trust and loyalty could arise from practical helpfulness and reliable conduct that were not immediately transactional. Advice, responsiveness, and the ability to solve the customer's actual problem could create a relationship that continued beyond the individual purchase. Evidence: professional ethnography — returning customers and non-transactional assistance

Integrated multi-role professional and project practice

Across different projects, professional involvement repeatedly extended beyond a narrowly defined position or single specialist function. The recurring pattern was not simply holding several job titles. It involved connecting functions that a project needed and, in some cases, taking responsibility for the entire chain from initial conception through implementation and ongoing operation.

Form of practiceExamples and scope
End-to-end business and operational responsibilityThe Christmas-market venture involved comprehensive preparation and operation: organizing the commercial activity, sourcing and assortment, practical setup, sales, and the connected operational work. It was independently organized and operated, while the relevant trade authorization was held through the brother's business. This distinction separates practical project ownership from the formal holder of the authorization. Evidence: professional ethnography — Christmas-market venture
End-to-end independent professional practiceFreelancing developed into a self-directed professional activity encompassing client relationships, scoping and planning, research, SEO, copywriting, content work, and the coordination needed to deliver client assignments. Self-employment from 2013 is a business-status milestone, not the beginning of all earlier copywriting or SEO activity. Evidence: professional ethnography — transition to freelancing and independent practice
Multi-role engagement within other projects and organizationsWork in family business, Studio CMYK, HOTJET, and the project Přílet ptáka Ludvíka involved combinations of content, communication, organization, editorial, technical, operational, and customer-facing tasks. The exact mix and degree of responsibility differed between projects. Evidence: professional ethnography — multi-role work in family business and external projects
Concept-to-implementation project developmentThe Student.idnes.cz proposal was developed in 2004 but did not launch then because the iDnes editorial team lacked implementation capacity. In 2009, a related but distinct student-media initiative, Stisk Studentský deník, appeared on iDnes through an FSS MU connection, with its own URL and development history. The genealogical connection is therefore a trajectory from concept and system design to a later, related realization—not an assertion that the two publications were the same project or output. Evidence: professional ethnography — Student.iDnes.cz concept and 2009 Stisk follow-on
End-to-end conceptual and research developmentDIO/ODI itself has developed as a comprehensive author-led research and conceptual undertaking, spanning problem articulation, theoretical and methodological work, models and taxonomies, publications, a reference model, digital infrastructure, applications, and dissemination. It is an ongoing project rather than a completed one; its scope is integrated, and its development is also recursive. Evidence: professional ethnography and its DIO/ODI changelog · DIO/ODI Hub

These examples differ in scope and in the degree of responsibility involved. They should not be flattened into an assertion that every collaboration was a complete project. Their common relevance lies in the recurring integration of functions and the ability to move between roles as the requirements of a project changed.

This pattern contributed to an understanding of projects as interconnected systems rather than collections of isolated tasks. It is one of the practical backgrounds to the later ability to connect conceptual, communicative, organizational, technical, and operational dimensions within a single framework.

Psychology, communication and academic inquiry

University studies at the Faculty of Social Studies of Masaryk University brought psychology into sustained contact with journalism, media and communication studies. These disciplinary strands developed alongside professional work and informed one another. Evidence: professional ethnography — university studies and disciplinary context

Psychological study provided tools for examining individual behaviour, motivation, social influence, cognition, and communication. The 2006 bachelor's thesis on key aspects of suicide cults from the individual's perspective combined psychological concepts with semiotic and ideological analysis. The work is an antecedent in the analysis of identity, authority, influence, group meaning, and information control; it is not presented as an early formulation of DIO. Evidence: IS MU institutional thesis record · full-text thesis · registry research-object record

A particularly important methodological development appears in the 2009 psychology thesis on positive psychology and Castaneda's teaching. Gregory Bateson's principle of binocular vision was used to compare two related but differently coded perspectives. The purpose was not to claim that the perspectives had equivalent epistemic status. Their differences were used as an analytical resource: reconstructing and comparing each perspective could produce additional depth and information that neither supplied on its own. Evidence: IS MU — successfully defended second submission · full text and DOI · registry thesis-lineage record

This work is significant not only because of the specific theories examined, but also because of the methodological stance it demonstrates. Methodology was not treated merely as a collection of established procedures to memorize and reproduce. It became a way of learning how to construct an appropriate approach to a problem, select relevant perspectives, and justify how they could be brought into productive relation. Evidence: the thesis's methodological comparison and binocular-perspective procedure · professional ethnography — positive psychology, Bateson and Castaneda

The 2005 semiotic analysis of the mOBSCENE video and the 2011 thesis on the rearticulation of ideology and hegemony provide complementary examples of analytical reconstruction. The former traces the expansion of meaning from individual signs through denotation, connotation, and broader interpretive structures. The latter reconstructs established concepts and reorganizes them into an analytically functional framework. Evidence: 2005 thesis bibliographic record · 2005 thesis full text · 2011 thesis — IS MU record · 2011 thesis full text · mOBSCENE registry record · rearticulation registry record

These works document related but distinct strands: cross-perspectival inquiry, semiotic reconstruction, discourse analysis, and conceptual rearticulation. They help explain the later capacity to work across disciplinary boundaries without treating all perspectives or methods as interchangeable. Evidence: semiotics and signification thread · communication, discourse and power thread · structuralism and critical-discourse thread

The practical psychology placements added situated exposure to several institutional environments, including police recruitment and personnel psychology, a psychiatric clinic, marriage and family counselling, and educational-psychological counselling. Their relevance is the experience of encountering different systems of assessment, communication, and support in practice, not a claim of subsequent licensed psychological practice. Evidence: professional ethnography — practical psychology placements

From copywriting to structural-semantic digital communication

Copywriting and web work gradually extended beyond the production of individual texts. Work associated with Studio CMYK and HOTJET, approximately in the 2004–2006 period, combined writing and editing with translation, press and public-relations work, website editing, content management, databases, graphic materials, product information, and technical documentation. The precise mix of responsibilities varied across assignments. Evidence: professional ethnography — Studio CMYK and HOTJET assignments

Experience with journalism and media studies also brought attention to the formal organization of digital communication. HTML could be understood not merely as technical code, but as a structured language through which headings, lists, labels, links, metadata, and other elements contributed to how content was organized and interpreted. Evidence: professional ethnography — early SEO and semantic HTML practice

This created a bridge between copywriting and technical web practice. Meaning was not treated as residing only in the visible prose. It was also conveyed through document structure, naming, metadata, internal and external links, image descriptions, and the context in which a page appeared. Evidence: professional ethnography — HTML structure, on-page elements and internal linking · 2012 SEO glossary — archived terminology and practice

From 2013, independent SEO and copywriting developed into a sustained self-employed practice, alongside professional collaborations and projects involving larger bodies of content. Work in these environments extended the combination of information accuracy, audience-oriented writing, semantic structure, and practical technical implementation. Evidence: professional ethnography — independent practice from 2013 and subsequent projects

Later experiments on danielberanek.cz extended this work into content migration, historical archiving, and forms of semantic syndication. In the latter, texts published on external platforms were contextually referenced from the author's own websites through separately formulated introductions, headlines, and other textual framing. This belongs to a later phase of practice and is not attributed to the 2012 Webnode material. Evidence: professional ethnography — later work on danielberanek.cz and semantic syndication

Pre-DIO professional engagement with AI

A further transitional record is the article Marketing pro AI startupy & SaaS: copywriting a SEO pro vývojáře LLM a aplikací umělé inteligence. It brings the preceding professional strands—SEO, copywriting, journalism and PR, psychology, and commercial experience—into direct contact with AI products and LLM-based services. Its perspective is reciprocal: it considers both how professional communication expertise can help AI developers explain and position their products, and how AI systems can extend professional information work while introducing practical limitations.

The earliest currently documented online state is an Internet Archive capture from 12 March 2025, corroborated by an Archive.today copy. The exact original publication date is not established. The live page's byline shows 21 October 2026 as its latest update, not as the article's first publication date. This article is therefore treated as a pre-DIO professional and conceptual precursor, not as an early formulation of DIO or ODI. The professional ethnography remains the primary research object from which DIO was explicitly articulated.

Evidence: Internet Archive — 12 March 2025 capture · Archive.today corroborating copy · current article page and 21 October 2026 update · registry research-object record.

Communication optimum and the digital representation of an entity

A particularly clear early articulation of this way of thinking is preserved in the project “SEO webu pro bábu i dědu z Ostravy i Chebu v neděli i středu”. The website remains available at seo-levou-zadni.webnode.cz; its historical content is documented by the March 2012 archive capture and the August 2012 capture. Evidence: SEO Webnode research-object record

The project extended beyond a document-centred conception of SEO, in which the individual webpage is the primary object of optimization. Its content addressed the broader consequences of how an entity is digitally represented: how the representation is interpreted, how the trustworthiness of the represented entity is assessed, and how these interpretations affect its commercial potential and relationships. Evidence: 2012 SEO glossary · 2012 SEO Errors page · registry record and provenance

A particularly significant formulation appears in the SEO glossary:

“Optimalizace webu je proces identifikace a realizace komunikačního optima – tj. stavu minimální rozpornosti jednak pro vyhledávače, jednak pro čtenáře.”

Primary source: SEO glossary — 30 August 2012 archive capture. The 17 March 2012 capture documents the earlier page state, before this definition appears.

The definition frames optimization as the pursuit of communication with minimal contradictions across different interpreters. The archived glossary captures from 17 March 2012 and 30 August 2012 show two different content states: the earlier snapshot does not yet contain the communication-optimum definition, while the later one does. This establishes that the definition appeared in the archived page state sometime within that interval; it does not establish the exact date on which it was formulated or first published. Evidence: date evidence and provenance record

Logical and semantic errors in communication

The Chyby SEO (“SEO Errors”) archive capture separates technical problems from logical and semantic errors in communication. Examples include outdated news sections, misleading labels, language that does not fit the intended audience, and grammatical or stylistic inconsistencies. The archived text describes how a contradiction in the user's experience produces cognitive discomfort that is attributed to the originator of the message. Although the term is not explicitly used there, the described mechanism corresponds to cognitive dissonance: the negative evaluation of the contradictory experience is transferred from the communication to the entity behind it.

The consequence is not confined to the individual webpage. The archived text explicitly connects these contradictions with the perceived trustworthiness of the message originator and its transactional potential. A defect in a representation can therefore affect the evaluation of the represented entity. Evidence: source object's archive and content record

Trustworthiness and the represented entity

The same page identifies physical existence, legal existence, and transaction history as dimensions relevant to the trustworthiness of the subject behind a website. This distinguishes the quality of a digital representation from the actual existence and activity of the entity it represents. The treatment of metadata makes a related distinction: an element such as author can have an attribution function even where its direct search-ranking value is considered negligible.

The project thus addresses more than how an individual document can be optimized. It considers how communication, attribution, evidence, and consistency shape the interpretation of the subject represented through that document.

Multiple representations and the communication whole

The glossary's definition of satellite websites refers to individual sites oriented towards a broader “communication whole” that supports a principal website and brand. This is an early formulation of the idea that a subject's digital presence need not be confined to one webpage or domain. Different representations can have distinct functions while contributing to a wider communicative whole.

The treatment of keywords across URLs, titles, metadata, headings, text, links, images, and descriptions in the archived glossary also reflects a cross-representational view of semantic signals. Its distinction between general search engines, vertical search services, and industry directories further shows attention to the context and intent of visitors rather than traffic volume alone.

The 2012 material therefore documents a conceptual shift: from optimizing a webpage as an isolated document towards considering the entity represented through it, the consistency of its communications, the evidence supporting its trustworthiness, and the relationships affected by its interpretation. Evidence: archival source record and interpretive classification

Reflexive experimental practice on self-owned objects

A recurring methodological pattern across the different areas of practice is the use of self-owned projects as environments for inquiry and practical experimentation. The object is not selected as a convenient example after a theory has already been completed. It is a familiar project on which interventions can be made, observed, evaluated, and refined over time. Evidence: professional ethnography — own-project research and iterative practice · primary research-object record

This practice has several connected levels:

  • Within-object iteration: a project is repeatedly adjusted in response to observed outcomes and accumulated experience.
  • Transfer across objects: insights from one self-owned project are carried into another, adapted to its circumstances, and tested again.
  • Recursive application: the conceptual framework itself, including its authorial and methodological foundations, becomes an object of examination and further development.

The author's own websites provide clear examples: the SEO Webnode site is not only a place where SEO is described, but also an environment in which content, structure, metadata, and technical implementation are applied and adjusted; danielberanek.cz provides a later, continuing environment for the same kind of practice. The later application of DIO/ODI to the author's own professional identity and to the DIO/ODI concept itself extends the same pattern recursively, as documented in the DIO/ODI Hub. Evidence: 2012 Webnode research object · professional ethnography record

The practice does not imply complete control over the environment. Search engines, platforms, customers, visitors, and AI systems remain independent actors. Their behaviour can provide feedback, but the primary object of intervention is the self-owned project, not the customer or visitor as an experimental subject. Evidence: professional ethnography — methodological and reflexive context · DIO/ODI as a continuing author-led project

This distinguishes reflexive experimental practice from integrated multi-role project work. The latter concerns the breadth of functions and responsibilities involved in a project; the former concerns how knowledge is generated, tested, accumulated, and transferred through repeated work on an object.

Emergent genealogical principles

The historical strands above give rise to a set of recurring principles. They describe how the way of thinking that led to DIO/ODI developed; they are not a retrospective rewording of the contemporary DIO/ODI architecture.

  1. Contextual and Intent-Oriented Understanding. Understanding people and their needs through behaviour, circumstances, indirect signals, and communicative context rather than relying exclusively on explicit requests. Evidence: retail and customer-interaction account · 2012 account of audience mismatch and communicative contradiction
  2. Cross-Perspectival Synthesis. Using relevant but differently coded perspectives to obtain analytical depth and insights unavailable from either perspective alone. Evidence: 2009 thesis — binocular-perspective procedure · professional ethnography — transfer of the method into practice
  3. Situated Systems Inquiry. Examining systems through direct engagement with their actual operation, internal relationships, and treatment of people and information, rather than relying solely on external descriptions or procedural checklists. Evidence: retail, multi-role business work and practical psychology placements
  4. Autonomous Analytical and Conceptual Construction. Developing methods in response to specific problems, reconstructing complex systems of meaning, and rearticulating inherited concepts into analytically functional frameworks. Evidence: 2005 semiotic analysis · 2011 concept rearticulation thesis · 2009 methodological thesis
  5. Structural-Semantic Conception of Communication. Understanding meaning as a result not only of textual content but also of structure, markup, labels, metadata, relationships, and the wider communicative environment. Evidence: early semantic HTML and on-page practice · 2012 glossary and communication-optimum definition
  6. Evidence-Grounded Relational Trust. Recognizing that trust depends on the relationship between communicated representations, actual conduct, supporting evidence, and the cumulative experience of interaction. Evidence: 2012 trustworthiness and transaction-history discussion · retail account of repeated customer relationships
  7. Integrated Multi-Role Project Work. Connecting multiple professional functions within a project and, where applicable, taking end-to-end responsibility from conception through implementation and operation. This includes both complete project ownership and multi-role engagement within other projects, without conflating their different scopes. Evidence: multi-role professional and project history · registry practice-thread record
  8. Reflexive Experimental Practice on Self-Owned Objects. Using self-owned projects as environments for intervention, observation, evaluation, iterative refinement, and transfer of knowledge across objects, including recursive application to the framework itself. Evidence: professional ethnography — own-project experimentation and iterative development · DIO/ODI Hub — recursive application

These principles are interconnected rather than independent doctrines. Some describe ways of understanding people and systems; others describe how methods and concepts are constructed; and others describe how knowledge is generated through practical work. Their historical expressions vary by context, but together they explain the convergence of professional, intellectual, and methodological experience that preceded DIO/ODI.

Convergence and DIO/ODI articulation

The available historical material does not indicate that DIO/ODI was a predetermined endpoint of the earlier activities. Its articulation followed a retrospective examination of a long-standing body of professional and intellectual experience. The professional ethnography became the primary object through which those previously distributed strands could be examined together. Evidence: primary research-object record

The author-maintained changelog records the articulation of Digital Identity Optimization on 21 May 2026 and the subsequent articulation of Ontology of Digital Identity as the broader theoretical layer on 22 June 2026. These dates mark the documented conceptual articulation, not the beginning of every underlying experience or idea. The professional-ethnography changelog records these milestones; the DIO/ODI Hub preserves the resulting conceptual corpus.

The resulting framework extends concerns that had previously appeared in different settings: interpreting needs in context, comparing distinct perspectives, examining whole systems, reconstructing and rearticulating meaning, grounding trust in evidence, integrating multiple functions, and developing knowledge through iterative work on self-owned objects. Evidence: the research-object and intellectual-thread registry · the DIO/ODI conceptual corpus

The important transition is not simply from SEO to AI. It is a change in the scope of the object being considered: from the individual document or webpage towards the entity represented through multiple digital traces, and towards the conditions under which those representations can be interpreted coherently and used to reconstruct an identity. Evidence: 2012 communication-optimum and multiple-representation antecedent · DIO/ODI articulation and reference materials

The genealogy of DIO/ODI is therefore the history of a gradually converging mode of inquiry: grounded in human interaction and practical systems, sharpened through psychological and communication research, extended through structural-semantic work on digital media, and progressively articulated through integrated project work, reflexive experimentation, and conceptual reconstruction.
